# Motivation Letter — International Assignment (EOS)

**Subject:** Interest in an international assignment during my dual studies

Dear [Name],

I would like to put my name forward for an international assignment as part of my dual study programme.

My placements at EOS have covered four distinct areas: case handling, support for EOS Field Service, Cloud Services (AWS and data), and Finance (forecasting). Seeing the business from four angles has been the most valuable part of my studies so far, from the daily reality of a case to the infrastructure the data sits on and the figures used in planning. It confirmed the direction I want to take: data engineering and forecasting, cloud technologies, and sensible process automation.

An international assignment would give me a second point of reference. So far I have only seen receivables-management processes in Germany. EOS is present in more than 20 countries, with different market conditions, regulatory environments and client sectors behind that. Seeing how another EOS company approaches similar challenges — and where it deliberately takes a different route — would show me more about how group-wide processes hold up across local contexts than another placement in Hamburg could. Both countries under discussion, Spain and Romania, would work for me for the same reason: our 2025 European Payment Practices study puts them among the front-runners in largely or fully digitised dunning processes, with Germany still some way behind.

I would also like to find out how I work in a team where English is the working language and where practices and assumptions differ from the ones I have taken for granted here. Discussing a data or process question with colleagues whose regulatory environment I do not yet know means asking better questions instead of treating the German setup as the default, and I would rather build that habit now than later. A dual study programme is the one phase where a move like this is part of my development rather than a break from it.

On my side, I would bring practical knowledge of how our processes run, a working understanding of the data and AWS environment behind them, and the habit of understanding why a process works the way it does before proposing changes to it. I would also come back with a concrete comparison rather than a general impression, which is something I could put to use directly in data and process work here.

Best regards,
Felix Heinsius
